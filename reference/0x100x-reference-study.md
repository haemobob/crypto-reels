## Cross-video motion grammar (grounded in frames)

1. **Microcaption clock**: One tiny narrated word at a time around the upper center. It is intentionally not the same role as the main title or diagram.
2. **Concept-as-object**: Each factual noun has a physical or diagrammatic counterpart (coin stacks, corporate card, simplified computer, house, port, timeline, bank).
3. **Single visual thesis per beat**: Each composition has one central statement, illustration or number; the visual subject often takes only 30–50% of vertical real estate.
4. **Scale-to-context**: A massive anchor statistic is followed by a comparative referent, often physically (piles, map, globe, monument).
5. **Quiet camera**: Medium-wide, portrait-appropriate virtual framing, with restrained dolly/orbit and strong negative space. Cuts and object motion often do more work than camera acrobatics.
6. **Narration-synchronous build**: Objects, numbers and key labels appear only when spoken. Avoid showing the whole mechanism prematurely.
7. **Explicit thesis breaks**: Black background, centered white line, little or no imagery for a crucial pivot; avoids information saturation.
8. **Semantic polarity changes**: Black/off-white shifts signal a fresh premise, time period, counterargument or reveal. Sampled flip points differ between videos; there is no constant 10-second rule.
9. **Transition diversity**: Hard cuts and polarity flips coexist with cards folding/reframing, counters, match cuts, data-flow continuity and spatial entrance. Do not force every boundary into a flashy portal or 3D morph.
10. **3D used sparingly**: Reflective bevelled tokens, pedestals, spheres and clean extruded icons; precise 2D type and charts remain majority of visual information.
11. **Human recognition cues**: Actual portrait/brand mark where authority credibility matters, not random faceless avatars for news figures.
12. **Argument rhythm**: Authority → scale → counterintuitive idea → old-world mechanics → historical analogy → new mechanism → market implication → payoff/CTA. Individual videos differ; never force inaccurate investment theses.
