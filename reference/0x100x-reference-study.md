## Cross-video motion grammar (grounded in frames)

1. **Microcaption clock**: One tiny narrated word at a time around the upper center. It is intentionally not the same role as the main title or diagram.
2. **Concept-as-object**: Each factual noun has a physical or diagrammatic counterpart (coin stacks, corporate card, simplified computer, house, port, timeline, bank).
3. **Single visual thesis per beat**: Each composition has one central statement, illustration or number; the visual subject often takes only 30–50% of vertical real estate.
4. **Scale-to-context**: A massive anchor statistic is followed by a comparative referent, often physically (piles, map, globe, monument).
5. **Quiet camera**: Medium-wide, portrait-appropriate virtual framing, with restrained dolly/orbit and strong negative space. Cuts and object motion often do more work than camera acrobatics.
6. **Narration-synchronous build**: Objects, numbers and key labels appear only when spoken. Avoid showing the whole mechanism prematurely.
7. **Explicit thesis breaks**: Black background, centered white line, little or no imagery for a crucial pivot; avoids information saturation.
8. **Semantic polarity changes**: Black/off-white shifts signal a fresh premise, time period, counterargument or reveal. Sampled flip points differ between videos; there is no constant 10-second rule.
9. **Transition diversity**: Hard cuts and polarity flips coexist with cards folding/reframing, counters, match cuts, data-flow continuity and spatial entrance. Do not force every boundary into a flashy portal or 3D morph.
10. **3D used sparingly**: Reflective bevelled tokens, pedestals, spheres and clean extruded icons; precise 2D type and charts remain majority of visual information.
11. **Human recognition cues**: Actual portrait/brand mark where authority credibility matters, not random faceless avatars for news figures.
12. **Argument rhythm**: Authority → scale → counterintuitive idea → old-world mechanics → historical analogy → new mechanism → market implication → payoff/CTA. Individual videos differ; never force inaccurate investment theses.

## Cryptonite-specific adaptation for the MSCI episode

- **0–5 s**: `MSCI` and `INDONESIA` with a singular large headline, not a 3D corridor.
- **5–11 s**: MSCI / Indonesia real-word cards and a large `EMERGING` anchor. Derive from the Buffett portrait-and-counter technique.
- **11–21 s**: Conceptual 3-level diagram *with a crucial accuracy correction*: Standalone is outside Emerging/Frontier index families, not literally the lowest standardized rung. Showing a drop past Frontier is permissible only when labeled *conceptual illustration / MSCI proposal*, not an actual geographic/global hierarchy.
- **21–28 s**: Black thesis interruption: `BUKAN TURUN SATU TINGKAT.` then `TERPISAH DARI DUA INDEKS.` Do not make unsupported claims about every MSCI index product.
- **28–41 s**: Three minimal graphics: opaque ownership web → alleged coordinated trading paths → enforcement/reform documentation. Progressive build timed to each accusation with cautious labels.
- **41–53 s**: Index basket into portfolio rebalancing flow. Crucial distinction: funds tracking affected indices may need to sell/rebalance *if* effective. Do not assert all investors are forced sellers.
- **53–62 s**: Date timeline (April 30 feedback and June 30 decision) growing across the screen; preserve the `BELUM FINAL` disclaimer.
- **62–70 s**: Final question on black, then Cryptonite logo with restrained blue highlight.

Aim for **60–75 seconds**. Let narration duration determine exact timings; avoid over-cramming a fixed 70-second cut. Maximize clarity rather than ornamental continuity.

## Non-negotiable visual constraints

- 1080×1920 output / 30fps; UI safe area.
- Base palette: #000000, #EDEDED, #050B18, with #1677FF/#2997FF/#75C7FF as the identity accents; red only for negative status and green for positive system states.
- Deliberate whitespace; no gratuitous glowing networks, stacks of random icons or animation on every pixel.
- Pair one-word upper microcaption with an independent central primary visual; disappear microcaption during clean thesis interruptions when necessary.
- Every significant claim has a timestamped visual and source in `sources.md`.
- Source files reviewed at 720×1280; this is the **reference resolution**, not the target resolution.
- When using authentic footage, clear usage rights. Recreate the style and methodology, never trace original branded visual compositions.
- Actual footage should be rendered and reviewed, including transition midframes and frame boundaries. Never claim motion has been validated from stills alone.

**Their language, distilled:**

2. **Monochrome plus one accent** (green). Other colours only where they carry meaning: bitcoin orange, China's red, a red X, a red mailbox.
3. **Typography in two registers.** Small, medium-weight sans for headlines and sentences. A tall condensed display face only for the big number.
4. **Characters and analogies.** Sphere-people act out the old way (the letter, the post office) before the new mechanism. A new visual idea arrives with almost every sentence.
5. **Continuous micro-motion.** Glints on edges, floating objects, ticking counters, a camera that pulls or pushes in every shot.
6. **Transitions:** dips to black, white flashes, hard polarity cuts, objects morphing (sphere → house → loan card), letter-by-letter type.
