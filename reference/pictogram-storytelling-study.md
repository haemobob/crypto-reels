## The shared visual language: ISOTYPE-style pictogram actors
- **Actors are pictograms:** a white person icon (round head, rounded shoulders) standing on a thin ground line.
  - They have no face, so they're everyone: relatable without being a real person and without casting.
  - A role is given by **one prop or icon plus a small label pill**: rings + "COUPLE", a pot + "CATERER", a flower + "FLORIST", a guitar + "BAND", a house + "PAINTER", a ball + "PRO FOOTBALLER", a chart + "TOP QUANT", a building + "JANE STREET".
- **Relationships are lines.**
  - Thin curved connectors draw from one actor to the next as the narration names them (the planner → caterer, florist, band).
  - **Money is dots travelling along the lines** toward whoever gets paid (A), so who pays whom can be watched.
- **Crowds are data.**
  - B fills a circle with footballer icons while the counter runs 0 → 130,000 ("each icon = 470 players").
  - A dot grid with one lit dot shows "0.4% get in".
  - A building's lit windows show 3,500 employees.
- **Counters roll with the spoken number** every time ($1,750 → $9,400; 0 → 130,000; 0 → $2.7M) and land exactly on the word.
- **Comparisons are simple objects:**
  - two bars ("9× harder than Harvard");
  - work vs finding buyers;
  - a balance scale that tips at the punchline (FAMOUS vs RICH);
  - house counters (0, 1, 2 vs 5, 15, 25).
- **Palette:**
  - A: pure black and white, plus emphasis in *italic serif* inside a sans sentence ("makes *more*", "didn't *cook*", "*finding* the people").
  - B: black and white plus **one neon accent (yellow) with a soft glow**, reserved for the money/quant side.
  - Grey marks the "other" actors.
- **Type has two tracks:**
  - **A: a sentence builder.** The spoken sentence assembles word by word at the top third. Key words switch style: CAPS bold ("BRINGS", "DOWNSTREAM.", "PIPELINE.") or italic serif. The previous sentence blurs out as the next begins.
  - **B: small-caps pills plus one huge number or word.** "WHAT'S MORE LIKELY?", "THE REWARD", then "$2,700,000" or "QUANT" in big glowing caps.
- **Camera:** one continuous canvas.
  - A pans sideways and reframes as new actors arrive at the edge; old ones slide off. The "camera" is a 2D pan/zoom.
  - B rebuilds each beat from the ground line: the line draws, objects rise, then a dip to black.
- **Entrances:**
  - icons pop in inside a briefly glowing ring (a "spotlight" that says *this one*);
  - lines draw on;
  - labels pop as pills;
  - numbers roll.
  No fades-and-slides.
- **The ending loops.** A's last shot rebuilds the first one (the network, "$9,400", now tagged PIPELINE), so the video loops seamlessly into its own hook: replays count as watch time.

## Storytelling structures

### A: rule → worked example → "it's everywhere" → why → parable → lesson → loop
| t | Line | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 s | "The person who brings the customer usually makes more than the person who does the job." | **Hook:** a contrarian rule, proved on screen at once by a money counter racing on the "brings the customer" actor |
| 5–7 s | "Watch how a wedding gets paid for." | **Promise of a story,** a familiar event |
| 7–17 s | couple → planner → caterer, florist, band → referral fee → "The planner didn't *cook* a thing." | **Worked example** acted by pictograms; the punchline lands on the italic word |
| 17–33 s | contractor keeps the difference · agent takes a share · creator gets paid per sale | **"It's the same everywhere":** three quick re-applications, each a new mini-scene |
| 33–42 s | "The hardest thing in any business isn't doing the work. It's *finding* the people who'll pay for it… controls everybody DOWNSTREAM." | **Why:** the mechanism, with bars (work vs finding buyers) and a tree |
| 42–62 s | "Picture two painters…" one paints, one runs a pipeline | **Parable,** two actors side by side, counters diverging |
| 62–69 s | "So learn to *bring* the work. Every trade pays the person who brings the customer *first*." | **Lesson,** plus a **callback loop** to the opening network |

### B: question duel → escalating evidence → mechanism → stakes → couplet punchline
| t | Line | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | "You're more likely to go pro in football than become a top quant." | **Hook:** a counterintuitive comparison with a universal reference (football); two actors face off |
| 4–11 s | 130,000 pro footballers · Jane Street, 3,500 employees total | **Evidence 1–2:** two counters, two visual scales (crowd vs building) |
| 11–16 s | Citadel internship 0.4% · 9× harder than Harvard | **Evidence 3–4:** a dot grid, two bars |
| 16–21 s | "No scouts, no academies, just MATH." | **Mechanism,** crossed icons, then one glowing word |
| 21–29 s | "The reward: $2.7 million, per person, per year. Most footballers: under a thousand a month." | **Stakes** for the two actors |
| 29–33 s | "Football makes you famous. Math makes you rich." | **Couplet punchline,** a balance scale tips |

### What makes them retain
1. **The first sentence is a claim the viewer has an opinion about**, and the picture proves it at once: a counter racing, two actors facing off.
2. **A story is promised and kept** ("Watch how a wedding gets paid for", "Picture two painters").
3. **Every sentence is a new visual idea, acted by the same kind of actor**, so the viewer never re-learns the visual language.
4. **Familiar worlds** carry an abstract idea: a wedding, a painter, football, Harvard.
5. **Numbers are events:** each one rolls in on its word, then a comparison makes it mean something.
6. **The ending is a line worth repeating** (a lesson or a couplet), and in A it loops into the hook.

## Cryptonite adaptation (rules)
- **A world of pictogram actors** for character-led episodes:
  - **Look:** black ground, white pictogram people on a ground line; grey for the other side; **brand blue is the one accent** (the side, the money or the key actor); red and green only where they carry meaning; a soft glow on the accent only.
  - **Indonesian, relatable roles:** karyawan, pedagang, ojol driver, mahasiswa, ibu rumah tangga, trader, the exchange, the bank, "bandar" (as a role, never a real person), the developer, the miner, the validator.
  - **Each role is one prop plus a pill label:** a phone with an exchange app, a motorbike, a warung, a laptop with a chart.
- **Money moves as dots along lines**, so who pays whom can be watched (fees, funding, liquidations, spreads).
- **Crowds as data** ("1 ikon = 10.000 orang"), with the ratio stated on screen.
- **Story templates** (pick per topic, record in `hooks.md`):
  1. **A rule, then a worked example** ("Lihat gimana satu transaksi di exchange menghasilkan uang buat mereka"), then "di mana-mana sama", then the why, then a parable, then the lesson and a loop.
  2. **A duel:** "Mana yang lebih mungkin: …?" then escalating evidence, the mechanism, the stakes, and a couplet punchline.
  3. **Two actors, two choices** (the HODLer vs the trader, DCA vs all-in), with counters diverging (illustration labelled SIMULASI) and a lesson.
- **Type:**
  - a sentence builder at the top third (word by word, the previous sentence blurs out);
  - the emphasis word in brand blue, or as an italic accent if an italic face is added;
  - small-caps pills for labels;
  - one huge rolling number at a time.
  - Micro captions are replaced by the sentence builder in this style: decide per episode in `episode.json`.
- **Camera:** one continuous 2D canvas that pans and zooms to the next actor. Dips to black only between chapters.
- **Pace:** the same gate (`npm run pace`): both references stay under 21% still, with no stretch over 1 s.
- **Ending:** the lesson line, then rebuild the opening shot so the Reel loops.
- **Truth still binds:** real numbers with sources; parables and diverging counters are labelled SIMULASI/ILUSTRASI; roles, never real people.

## What to build (Remotion, 2D SVG: fast and light)
- **`Actor`:** a pictogram person with pose variants (stand, walk, point, hold, sit, celebrate, slump), a prop slot, a label pill, an `active` glow ring, and grey/white/blue states.
- **`Link`:** a curved connector that draws on, with `Flow` dots travelling along it (direction, rate, colour).
- **`Crowd`:** an ISOTYPE grid or circle that fills to a count, with a "1 ikon = N" legend.
- **`Counter` / `RollNumber`** (exists), **`Pill`**, **`Bars`**, **`Scale`** (a balance that tips), **`DotGrid`** (x out of N).
- **`SentenceTrack`:** the word-by-word builder from `captions.json`, with emphasis by `*word*`.
- **`Canvas`:** a world-space 2D camera (pan/zoom keys on cues); actors placed in world coordinates.
