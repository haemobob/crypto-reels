# CRYPTONITE — VIEWER VALUE & RETENTION DIRECTIVE (17 Oct 2026)

This directive governs scripting decisions. It is applied **before** the V3 retention techniques (`cryptonite-retention.md`), the opening rules (`cryptonite-openings.md`) and the V2 visual rules (`cryptonite-reels.md`).

## The Cryptonite standard
Every Reel needs three things:
1. **A clear viewer benefit.** Before writing the script, answer: what will someone know, understand, or be able to do after watching? (Example: learning how to inspect an order book before buying an altcoin.)
2. **A sequence of satisfying discoveries.** Reveal the answer progressively: an early useful insight, a visual demonstration, a final takeaway. Each section resolves one question while naturally creating the next.
3. **A reason to remember or save it.** A practical check, mental model, meaningful comparison, or piece of information viewers can use afterwards. Saving and sharing are earned through usefulness, not requested through repetitive CTAs.

## Primary objective
Every Cryptonite Reel must provide meaningful educational value while maximising the likelihood that viewers keep watching. The objective is not to manufacture curiosity without satisfying it; it is to reward attention through useful discoveries.

## 1. Define the viewer reward first
Before writing any script, explicitly define:
- Who is this video for?
- What problem, question or misconception does the viewer have?
- What specific thing will the viewer learn?
- Why does this matter to an Indonesian crypto investor?
- What useful information will they remember afterwards?

The answer must be concrete.
- Weak: "The viewer learns about cryptocurrency."
- Strong: "The viewer learns how to identify two warning signs of low liquidity before buying an altcoin."

If the topic lacks a meaningful viewer reward, improve the angle or choose a stronger topic before production.

## 2. Never produce content just to fill time
Duration follows the information being taught. A great 35-second Reel beats a diluted 75-second one. Do not extend narration, insert filler or repeat explanations to reach a duration target. Default to the shortest runtime that delivers the complete educational payoff naturally.

## 3. Open with the most compelling entry point
Do not force every video to begin with the same type of hook. Choose the entry point that best serves the subject:
- **Authority:** a recognisable person or institution
- **Problem:** something viewers personally experience
- **Contradiction:** an outcome that seems illogical
- **Evidence:** a surprising verified number or observation
- **Simulation:** a concrete scenario that demonstrates the problem
- **Question:** something the audience genuinely wants answered

Prioritise credible specificity and audience relevance over exaggerated suspense.

## 4. Reward attention early
Within roughly the first 8–12 seconds, deliver the first meaningful piece of information. Do not delay the explanation by stacking introductory claims. The opening says why the story matters; the next section starts fulfilling that promise.

## 5. Progressive information delivery
Structure the script as connected discoveries. Each section:
1. answers an existing question,
2. reveals an important mechanism or implication,
3. naturally creates a reason to continue.

No arbitrary cliffhangers. Never withhold an answer that could be given earlier merely to stretch watch time. Use meaningful developments, not empty phrases such as "wait until the end".

## 6. Demonstrate, don't decorate
Motion graphics visually explain the spoken information. For example:
- Liquidity → actual orders and market depth
- Leverage → margin and liquidation
- Token supply → creation, distribution and unlocking
- ETF flows → money entering or leaving a fund
- Blockchain → a transaction travelling through verification

Every important motion graphic serves a specific educational purpose. Remove animations that do not improve comprehension, emphasis or flow.

## 7. Use real-world assets when they improve credibility
Prioritise relevant authentic assets: photographs, official logos, trading interfaces, market charts, research documents, news announcements, historical footage. Use them when appropriate, not automatically.

Do not fabricate evidence or present simulated graphics as actual historical market data. **Label simulations clearly** (on screen: "SIMULASI" / "ILUSTRASI"). If a required asset is unavailable, say exactly what is needed instead of silently substituting an inaccurate illustration.

## 8. Design for sound-off comprehension
A viewer understands the main idea from visuals and text without the narration: clear headlines, readable captions, strong hierarchy, specific charts and diagrams, progressive labels, visible cause and effect. Do not overcrowd the screen.

## 9. End with a real payoff
Every educational video resolves its central promise. The ending gives viewers something memorable: a practical check, a clear mental model, an important distinction, a useful warning, or a meaningful conclusion. Do not force investment advice or a hidden-winner narrative where the evidence does not justify one. **A CTA is optional; the educational payoff is mandatory.**

## 10. Retention quality gate (before animation begins)
- Is the opening understandable within three seconds?
- Does the audience have a reason to care?
- Is there a meaningful early payoff?
- Does each section contribute new information?
- Is the central mechanism explained clearly?
- Can any sentence be removed without reducing understanding?
- Is the final insight worth remembering?
- Would the viewer feel the video delivered what it promised?

If the script fails, rewrite it before producing motion graphics.

## 11. Post-publication learning
Use actual Instagram metrics to improve later videos. Track where available: three-second retention or view rate, average watch time, watch-time distribution or retention curve, completion rate, saves, sends/shares, audience feedback. Identify whether viewers leave during the opening, the explanation or the conclusion, and test changes to the weakest part of the story. Do not assume more motion, longer videos or more sensational language automatically improve retention.

## Pre-production concept scorecard
Score every concept before scripting goes further (recorded in `retention-brief.md`). Each criterion 1–5:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Viewer relevance | Would an Indonesian crypto investor care? |
| Educational payoff | Does the viewer leave with a useful insight? |
| Retention structure | Is there a clear reason to watch each section? |
| Credibility | Are facts and examples accurate and properly framed? |
| Visual explanation | Do the graphics clarify how something works? |

Concept score = sum × 4 (out of 100). Default bar: rework the angle when any criterion is below 3, or when Educational payoff or Credibility is below 4. Creative scores are filters; the final judgment comes from how real viewers respond (`performance-review.md`).

## Final principle
The audience gives Cryptonite its attention; every Reel gives something valuable in return. Storytelling earns the next second. Visual design makes the idea easier to understand. The final takeaway makes the video worth remembering.

Every video starts with a viewer problem or meaningful discovery, explains a mechanism using credible real-world assets, and ends with something the audience can actually use.
