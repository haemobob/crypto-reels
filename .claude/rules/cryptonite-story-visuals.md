# Cryptonite story-driven visual direction (17 Oct 2026)

The story determines the visuals. The visuals serve the explanation. Retention comes from delivering value.

Never default to the same person, institution, asset or visual protagonist across different Reels. The examples in the other rule files (Larry Fink and BlackRock, the order book, the MSCI building) are illustrations of a technique, not subjects to reuse.

## For every new topic
1. Research the story and identify its most recognisable, relevant subjects.
2. Decide whether a real person, asset, institution, event or financial mechanism makes the strongest opening.
3. Select the visual subject for its actual relevance to the narrative.
4. Use authentic photographs, logos, documents, market visuals or realistic objects where appropriate.
5. Build the creative direction around what is unique about this story.
6. Change compositions, camera framing, environments and transitions between episodes so videos don't repeat.
7. Let important subjects reappear when they help narrative continuity (a follow-up story, a recurring institution).
8. Never insert famous personalities only to generate attention.
9. Never force a central character when the topic is better explained through data or diagrams.

## How this fits the other rules
- The opening's "real-world anchor" (`cryptonite-openings.md`) is whatever is genuinely central to *this* story: a person, a document, a chart, a trading interface or a dataset. For a mechanism-led topic the anchor can be real data or a real interface, with no person at all.
- 0x100x (`reference/0x100x-reference-study.md`) is a reference for storytelling quality, visual restraint, cinematic composition and information delivery, not a fixed formula or shot list.
- What stays constant across episodes: typography, blue branding, the Cryptonite logo and outro, captions, and production quality. Everything else (subject, visual concept, environments, compositions, hero move, transitions) is designed per story.

## Record it
In `brief.md`, before the visual plan:
- **Visual subject** and why it is the most relevant one for this story (or why data/diagrams carry it instead of a character).
- **Visual concept**: the one idea the look is built around, specific to this story.
- **Different from recent episodes**: check `reference/episode-visual-log.md` and name what changes (subject, environment, composition, hero move, transitions). Reuse is fine only when it serves continuity, and say so.

After the episode is built, add its row to `reference/episode-visual-log.md`.
