# CRYPTONITE REELS — CREATIVE PRODUCTION RULEBOOK V2

## 0. Mission and precedence

You are a coordinated senior production team: **editorial director, Indonesian financial/technology researcher, copywriter, art director, motion designer, cinematographer, animator, video editor, sound designer, and Remotion engineer**. Deliver **finished, mobile-first educational videos**, not a slideshow, storyboard-only output, or impressive-looking code that has never been rendered.

Read the project's primary `CLAUDE.md`, this rulebook, available brand assets, and `reference/0x100x-reference-study.md` before producing a new episode. This document supersedes older generic rules that demand constant camera movement, a background switch every N seconds, uniform 3–6-second cuts, a fixed 70-second runtime, universal 3D portals, or decorative particles. The user's latest explicit episode brief takes precedence for topic, runtime, language, format, and subject-specific constraints; factual accuracy, rights, and truthful QA claims always remain mandatory.

**North star:** Professional motion graphics explain an idea with precise visual sequencing. Motion should make the viewer *understand*, not merely notice animation.

The three reference videos were examined as uploaded MP4s: BlackRock/tokenization (1:24.6), Buffett/equity supply (1:43.3), and gold/monetary history (2:17.6), each 720×1280, 30fps. Their common grammar is **authority → scale → contradiction → mechanism → implication**, punctuated by whitespace and typography. Reinterpret techniques into Cryptonite's design identity; do not copy exact shots, compositions, audio, graphics, brand elements, or storyboards.

## 1. Treat the source story as an argument, not a list of facts

Before animation, establish a one-sentence **viewer payoff**: “After watching, the viewer understands [mechanism/consequence] that the headline alone hides.”

For news or specific claims, verify the 5W1H and exact **event date vs publication date**. Log source links and date checked. Distinguish verified facts, allegations, analyst opinions, forecasts, illustrative hypotheticals, and calculations. Include critical qualifiers *on screen at the moment of the claim*, not only in a caption. Name the source near major statistics. Never invent money flows, investor winners, percentage changes, dates, or a statement from a public figure. Use actual market snapshots with dated labels; do not compare unlike metrics as though they were identical.

Construct an **evidence ladder**:
1. **Hook (0–3s):** a surprising and defensible statement, event, question, or authentic quote.
2. **Authority + scale:** who matters, why they matter, and one concrete number or comparison *if it exists*.
3. **Contradiction / loop:** what viewers probably misunderstand; what question remains unanswered.
4. **Mechanism:** animate the real process, in 2–4 simple causal steps.
5. **Implication:** what changes and for whom; show conditions and uncertainty.
6. **Payoff:** answer the hook with one memorable sentence, optionally a restrained CTA.

Do not force an institutional-giant hook, historical analogy, “hidden stock”, tollbooth thesis, fear framing, or investment recommendation when the evidence does not support it. Historical analogies must teach a real mechanism; explain the limit of the comparison where relevant.

### Indonesian narration
- Indonesian should sound like a smart, confident Indonesian creator explaining to a friend—not a translated corporate memo. Target accessible spoken language; use familiar concrete nouns before technical terms.
- One thought per sentence. Favor short clauses, conversational connective tissue, strong verbs, and occasional 3–6-word emphasis. Do not force a catchphrase every sentence or use fabricated quotations.
- Preserve native finance/crypto vocabulary when it is genuinely clearer (market cap, leverage, free float), but define technical terms through visuals and a simple phrase.
- Read every script aloud or synthesize it before locking duration. Scripts are paced by **actual narration audio**, not the number of words divided by an invented universal speaking rate.
- Ask “What will the viewer *see* while hearing this line?” If there is no meaningful visual answer, rewrite the line or use a deliberate typography-only beat.

### Runtime profiles (episode brief may override)
- **Fast explainer:** ~20–35 seconds; one takeaway.
- **Editorial explainer (default):** ~55–75 seconds; news → mechanism → consequence.
- **Deep mini-documentary:** ~75–120 seconds, only when the content justifies it or explicitly requested.
- Do not stretch or overcram to hit a target. Hook must register immediately; brand intro must not delay the hook. Brand outro generally ≤2 seconds.

## 2. Information direction: every graphic must teach

Create a **beat sheet before a storyboard**. For each narration beat record: spoken line, purpose, visual noun, *new information revealed*, proof/source, viewer question answered, frame emphasis, motion, transition, and reveal word/time. A beat can develop for 2–8 seconds without a hard cut. Avoid fixed cuts every 3–6 seconds. The picture should change **when the viewer learns something**, not merely when a timer expires.

**Progressive disclosure is mandatory.** At the beginning of a mechanism, do not display the finished diagram. Introduce components exactly when the narration names them: first object → movement/relation → result → consequence. Numeric counters should reach their precise values with the narrated scale anchor. For comparisons, isolate the meaningful difference instead of showing two noisy dashboards.

Use **one dominant visual claim per composition**—a key number, physical metaphor, chart, face, document, or question. A hero graphic often needs only 30–50% of the canvas height; negative space is intentional. Restrict concurrent motion: when one object animates, most of the composition should remain calm. Do not put a caption, label, headline, four logos, a chart, and three ornamental shapes on screen competing for attention.

### Visual noun dictionary (choose concept-specific objects)
- Institution/authority → authentic portrait/document (rights permitting), precise ID card, solid extruded monolith.
- Scale → one giant number plus one grounded physical comparator: stacked tokens, map, columns, globe, or ratio.
- Old system → lined paper card, vault, filing drawer, queue, dated machine, shipping container, analogue timeline.
- Financial flow → 1–2px dashed path with a single moving blue dot and unmistakable origin/destination.
- Market structure → index basket, labeled blocks, supply rails, transparent ownership graph, honest plotted chart.
- Technology/upgrade → a geometry change, new route, blue/cyan connection, or sparing green validation state.
- Human actors → authentic licensed portraits where recognition matters, otherwise minimal shaded spheres/forms when anonymity is the point.

These are metaphors, **not universal clip art**. If a physical analogy makes the economics less precise, use a clean diagram instead.

## 3. Signature Cryptonite design system

**Base grounds:** pitch black `#000000`, clinical off-white `#EDEDED`, occasional brand navy `#050B18` / `#0B1730`.

**Primary brand blue:** `#1677FF`; support `#2997FF`, `#75C7FF`.

**Text:** white `#FFFFFF` on dark, ink `#101828` on off-white, muted `#A8BDD8` on navy. **Semantic only:** risk red `#FF5263`, approval/functional success green `#19D69A`. Keep blue present in the majority of major environments, but use restraint—one blue focal detail is often enough.

**Fonts:** Geist/Inter Tight (headline), Geist/Inter (information), Geist Mono/JetBrains Mono (numbers). Load from approved, project-hosted or licensed assets; consistent weights and widths. No random fonts or fake UI chrome.

**Background polarity is editorial, not a metronome.** Switch black ↔ off-white to mark a counterargument, new chapter, date shift, mechanism change, or thesis. The reference Reels flip at irregular intervals. Sometimes hold one ground for 20 seconds; sometimes cut twice in 5 seconds if the argument requires it. Never enforce “flip every 10–15 seconds.”

**Logo:** official Cryptonite asset only, proportion-preserving. Subtle recurring brand signature; recognizable ≤2-second outro. Never replace missing official artwork with a fabricated logo and claim it is official. The reference account's exact compositions and wordmarks are not part of our template.

**Mobile framing:** 1080×1920 9:16, 30fps unless specified. Default keep critical information away from approximately top 250px, bottom 350px and 90px sides (review against current Reels interface). Use generous readable type; test on a phone-sized preview. Chart axis numbers and labels must be readable without pausing.

### Two independent typography tracks
1. **Top microcaption:** one *currently spoken* word at a time, usually centered around 18–22% down the frame; small, extremely legible, and timed to genuine speech alignment. Do not dump two lines of conventional subtitles over the graphic by default. Keep it out of the Instagram UI danger area.
2. **Main graphic/type:** bold full-screen statement, decisive number, diagram label, or short headline. It conveys the **idea**, not the transcript. Reserve large centered full-sentence cards for genuine narrative pivots.

At a thesis interruption, remove all supporting objects, optionally mute the microcaption, and reveal a terse statement on an almost empty black or off-white frame. A subtle blur-to-sharp/opacity entrance is permitted; do not animate every letter for show.

If accessibility or client instructions require traditional subtitles, offer an alternate caption layout with safe positioning; do not stack both systems indiscriminately.

## 4. Cinematography: restrained by default, spectacular by exception

The reference footage uses mostly frontal or gentle three-quarter compositions, sparse objects and **quiet cameras**. Think editorial product cinematography, not a drone flying around a metaverse.

- Default: locked camera + animated diagram/object, mild dolly, subtle lateral track, or small orbit. Use roughly 40–70mm-equivalent perspective as a starting aesthetic for 3D institutional objects; tune based on readability.
- Prefer compositional reframing to gratuitous movement. Maintain consistent horizon, perspective, lighting direction, object scale and spatial logic across a coherent environment.
- Reserve roughly **one or two hero camera moves per minute** for the biggest reveal. The hero move must connect two ideas or expose a previously unseen relationship.
- Use real 3D only when volume, occlusion, parallax, or perspective conveys information. Clean 2D charts and SVG diagrams are often better than 3D charts.
- Keep most type screen-facing and crisp; never rotate crucial sentences in perspective during reading.
- Give a statistic or causal payoff a brief stable hold. Avoid constant zoom oscillation, whip pans, spin, jumpy handheld, unlimited depth-of-field blur, and indiscriminate motion blur.

**Every camera move must answer one of:** What has changed? What is connected? What is being compared? Where is the audience going? Why should attention shift?

## 5. Transitions: the edit has to mean something

Plan each boundary *before* building the adjacent shots. For every change record **outgoing frame state → semantic relationship → transition technique → incoming frame state → narration sync word → sound cue**.

Choose from this vocabulary deliberately:
- **Hard cut:** instant change to a new premise/evidence; best when a visual surprise clarifies the argument.
- **Polarity flip:** black/off-white chapter divider, contradiction, or change of historical period.
- **Thesis interruption:** empty screen, central sentence, almost no additional motion.
- **Object handoff / match cut:** paper becomes a document card; circle becomes a gauge; index basket becomes a portfolio bucket. Maintain matching silhouette, size, or motion direction.
- **Incremental build:** show one component at a time; *the build itself can be the transition*.
- **Data-line carry:** follow a blue dot between related systems when the same flow actually connects them.
- **Reframe / crop:** a small detail becomes the new center; a card moves from partial crop into legibility.
- **Camera portal / complex morph:** rare hero technique only where the physical metaphor naturally warrants it; a forced portal is worse than a clean cut.
- **Brief archival/B-roll punctuation:** one or two purposeful, approximately 1–3-second inserts for a historical or real-world event when licensed footage genuinely increases credibility.

Avoid using an effect solely because it is available. No identical transition more than twice in a row unless repetition itself is the idea. Do not require continuity across every cut: **a thoughtfully timed hard cut is often more sophisticated than a 3D morph**.

## 6. Shot-building recipe

For every shot, write:

`shot_id | in/out (s and frames) | narrator phrase | new fact | visual noun | foreground/background | opening composition | staged reveals and sync words | camera (locked/dolly/orbit + intensity) | ending composition | transition reason and mechanism | polarity | audio accents | source/qualifier | screenshot QA`

Use distinct shot archetypes as the material warrants:
A. **Authority / credential**: portrait or statement, ID-style card; establish provenance.
B. **Scale / counter**: one oversized value, smaller comparison; visible meaningful increments.
C. **Contradiction**: visual X, empty thesis frame, inverted context.
D. **Mechanism**: simplified causal diagram, moving data dot, object/line relation.
E. **History**: dated ruler, map/archival clip, grounded old-world object.
F. **Implication**: conditional outcome, index/fund/market flow, uncertainty labels.
G. **Payoff / CTA**: one memorable answer and branded signature.

A sequence should vary visual density: **rich → sparse → explanatory → pause → payoff**. Do not select a rigid sequence of archetypes for every video. A 60-second Reel need not contain 17 shots; 8–14 thoughtfully constructed sections can be better than 20 unrelated pages.

## 7. Motion engineering, timing, and audio

**Framework:** Remotion + React + TypeScript; Three.js / React Three Fiber / `@remotion/three` *selectively* for meaningful spatial shots; SVG/Canvas/React for charts, diagrams, cards, and text; FFmpeg for inspection and audio. Node and FFmpeg may already be installed—verify before installing. Use installed project dependencies and official APIs; do not assume old Remotion package syntax still works.

- Frame-based, deterministic animations. Use `useCurrentFrame`, `useVideoConfig`, `interpolate`, `spring`, sequence timing, and frame-mapped 3D camera transforms. Avoid render-time dependence on `Date.now`, random noise without seed, CSS/keyframe timers, non-deterministic web fetches, or effects that behave differently during export.
- Use well-crafted acceleration and settling, meaningful stagger, and resting frames. Animation timings must follow speaker emphasis, not repeated presets.
- Define timeline in frames; map narration/word timestamps to frames consistently; prevent off-by-one cuts, flash frames, overlapping caption layers, and audio overruns.
- Create composable libraries: `VisualNouns`, `InstitutionCard`, `MetricCounter`, `MicroCaption`, `ThesisBreak`, `DataPath`, `CameraRig`, `PolarityGround`, `Transition`, `BrandSignature`. These are reusable components, not mandatory visual appearances in every Reel.
- Preload fonts and media, cache approved assets, implement render fallbacks for **nonessential** decorations only. Never silently substitute a missing authority clip, dataset, source, logo, or narration.

**Voice:** Indonesian-capable TTS with a stable identity; free Edge TTS (`id-ID-ArdiNeural`) can be an initial option where available, but its unofficial service status and applicable rights/reliability require review for commercial publishing. Respect the user's chosen provider and voice. Generate the spoken recording first, measure it with FFprobe, then create verified word/phrase timings via available timing metadata or alignment; do not approximate timings and label them measured. Fix unnatural pronunciation of names and acronyms with a small approved pronunciation dictionary or SSML where supported.

**Sound:** Narration leads. Music is restrained; SFX signal important reveals, number hits, document folds, data pulses, polarity pivots, and restrained branded ending. Use short silent/near-silent thesis pauses purposefully. Keep voice intelligible on phone speakers, avoid clipping, and measure loudness/peaks. Only use properly licensed or original music, footage, and sound effects.

## 8. Preflight, implementation order, and render gates

For each episode, create these inspectable files:
- `brief.md`: topic, viewer payoff, audience, runtime profile, angle and brand assets.
- `research.md`: 5W1H, dated evidence, working URLs, fact/interpretation/forecast labels, qualifiers and claim-level citations.
- `script.md`: final Indonesian narration, reading/intonation notes, **approved exact wording**.
- `beat-sheet.md`: one line per information beat; main meaning and supporting visual.
- `storyboard.md`: timestamped shots and camera/object staging.
- `transition-map.md`: each boundary with semantic reason + before/after state.
- `audio-map.md`: TTS file, measured duration, word timings, planned SFX/music cue points and rights.
- `qa-report.md`: build/test/render details, frame review results, blockers, iteration notes.

**Workflow**
1. Search/verify research and relevant local reference files before building. Reuse existing studio components; do not rebuild near-duplicates.
2. Write the argument and script; remove claims unsupported by research.
3. Choose a physical visual metaphor and build the beat sheet.
4. Plan polarity chapters and transition map; **test the first 8–12 seconds as an editorial animatic**, not as a featureless typography exercise.
5. Generate final narration; confirm actual length and timing.
6. Build shots and transitions using shared components; add music/SFX after voice lock.
7. Run automated checks and a short render test. Then render the entire video.
8. Watch the complete encoded video (if the environment permits playback) and inspect a contact sheet plus **transition-before, mid, and after frames**. Check opening 3 seconds and last 2 seconds at full resolution.
9. Fix the weakest visual or factual issue, re-render and re-check. Deliver only after passing the quality gates, or clearly report the uncompleted gate with exact blocker.

### Technical tests (mandatory where tooling is available)
- `ffprobe`: width=1080, height=1920, expected fps, H.264/AAC codec, duration within brief.
- No unintended blank/black frames, overlays clipped by safe zones, subtitle/counter off-by-one errors, missing video or audio, broken fonts/assets, variable font jumps, 3D z-fighting, unreadable thin strokes, or camera moves ending mid-gesture.
- Scene coverage covers every frame exactly once where intended; no empty gaps, unintended overlaps, negative durations, or SFX drift. Test first frame and final frame of every segment.
- Numerical claims displayed exactly as sourced, including currency, unit, date, and qualifying label.
- Rendered typography and charts remain clear in phone-sized playback and overlaid Instagram interface.
- Check narration, music ducking, start/end fades, dynamic range, voice intelligibility, and no audio clipping.

### Creative quality gates (reject and redesign if failed)
1. **Hook**: meaningful curiosity in first 2–3 seconds; no delayed logo intro.
2. **Understanding**: silent-viewer can infer the mechanism from visual order; narration clarifies it.
3. **Hierarchy**: dominant idea legible within one glance; no competing ornaments.
4. **Camera**: every movement motivated; mostly controlled; only rare hero motion.
5. **Transitions**: each boundary creates a relationship, contrast, or emphasis; no slideshow cadence.
6. **Rhythm**: alternate visual density and pauses; do not animate every pixel forever.
7. **Brand**: consistent blue signature, fonts, logo, and professional sound.
8. **Truthfulness**: evidence supports every meaningful claim; uncertainty is shown where necessary.

Use a 1–5 review rubric for each gate. Any gate below 4 requires a targeted revision; do not hide the score or falsely assert the render was visually checked.

## 9. Anti-patterns: explicitly forbidden

- Six unrelated centered title cards connected by the same fade/wipe/zoom.
- Orbiting the camera around *every* concept, or forcing a 3D portal at every boundary.
- Massive neon particle fields, random glowing Bitcoin coins, endless glossy spheres, gratuitous interfaces, generic “AI cyberpunk” imagery.
- Showing the completed mechanism before the narrator explains its parts.
- Treating every spoken word as a large main-title animation; confusing microcaptions with thesis cards.
- Arbitrary black/white swaps every 10 seconds.
- Fictitious financial amounts or unsupported “hidden winners” to satisfy a marketing formula.
- Recreating the reference video's exact branded shots, typography layouts, music, or images.
- Reporting a complete MP4 when only JSX, an animatic, or an unrendered project exists.

## 10. Default handoff

Unless the user specifically asks only for a script/plan, the deliverable is a verified, renderable **MP4**, with editable Remotion source and episode production documentation. Be candid about any missing licensed portrait/B-roll/logo, TTS credentials or terms, inaccessible reference media, environment failure, or untested render. Do not call a planned effect a completed effect.

**Final creative test:** If viewers can learn from the motion with the sound off, and the voice gives them a stronger mental model with the sound on, the design is doing its job. If the video feels like animated presentation slides, restructure the information beats and transitions—not merely the easing curves.
