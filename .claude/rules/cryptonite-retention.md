# CRYPTONITE — RETENTION & HOOK LAB (V3)

## Precedence and goal

Read this alongside `.claude/rules/cryptonite-reels.md` and `reference/0x100x-reference-study.md`. This retention supplement takes precedence over older creative rules when they conflict with audience comprehension or story momentum. The user's explicit topic, desired length and constraints still take precedence. Never sacrifice facts, financial balance or source attribution for a sensational hook.

**North star: The viewer should know immediately why this story matters, receive its first reward quickly, and discover genuinely useful new information throughout. Cinematography supports the argument. The argument earns attention.**

Do not assume that longer videos inherently fail, short videos inherently succeed, or high watch time proves a hook worked. Compare per-video performance to matched Cryptonite videos and the available Instagram metrics.

## Rule 1 — Create the viewer-retention brief before any full script

For every episode produce `retention-brief.md` recording:
- **Audience:** exactly who should care (e.g., Indonesian stockholders, new crypto buyers, Bitcoin traders, everyday Indonesians).
- **Consequence:** one sentence answering 'Why should I spend a minute on this?'
- **Counterintuitive fact:** a verified surprise or contradiction, if the evidence supports one.
- **Evidence:** exact person/event/date/number that makes the premise credible.
- **One-sentence payoff:** what the audience will understand by the end.
- **Misconception:** what the audience might incorrectly believe.
- **Share trigger:** whom a viewer might naturally send this to and why, without forcing artificial share requests.
- **Emotional shape:** concern, curiosity, surprise, relief, or clarity; do not manipulate with fabricated fear.
- **Chosen length:** the shortest runtime that preserves the mechanism and payoff. Target 35–60 seconds for most single-idea experiments; use 60–90+ seconds only when the idea earns it or the user requests it.

Reject topics that offer only 'news happened' with no viewer-facing consequence or useful explanation. Reframe the angle rather than manufacturing stakes.

## Rule 2 — The Hook Lab is mandatory

Before writing a full script, develop **at least five meaningfully different Indonesian cold opens** from these categories:
1. **Specific consequence:** 'Kalau [event] terjadi, [real viewer-relevant consequence] bisa terjadi.'
2. **Authority + unusual action:** '[Recognizable authority] just said/did [verified surprising thing].'
3. **Contradiction:** 'Bukan [common assumption]. Justru [counterintuitive evidence].'
4. **Concrete scale or comparison:** '[number] [simple physical or financial comparison]—and here's what changes.'
5. **Visual simulation:** 'Bayangin kalau [plausible event]. [Visible chain reaction].'

Use at least three *different mechanisms*, not five synonyms for one clickbait headline. Do not force an authority hook where ordinary audiences do not recognize the authority. Don't put 'Halo', channel name, long background, generic scene-setting, 'di video ini', unexplained jargon, or a disclaimer monologue in the first three seconds.

Rate each candidate 0–5 on (a) personal consequence, (b) novelty/curiosity, (c) specificity, (d) evidence/trust, and (e) ability to represent it in one striking visual. Record scores and reasoning, choose the strongest aligned with target audience. This is an internal editorial rubric, **not a scientifically validated predictor of virality**. Write one alternative optimized for broader audiences and one for niche financially literate viewers. If the selected candidate has no genuine surprise, rewrite the story angle.

**First-frame test:** A non-follower should understand the stakes from the picture/main title alone in about one second. A one-word upper microcaption is NEVER the only opening text. Display an immediately readable focal headline or unmistakable visual event; narration begins within the first few frames. Brand/logo does not precede the hook.

## Rule 3 — The first 12 seconds are their own mini-story

Build and review a dedicated **0–12-second cold-open animatic** before committing to a whole episode:
- **0–1.5s: interrupt.** Present the main tension/consequence in a recognizable visual noun, credible face, contradictory symbol, or large clear title. No long establishing animation.
- **1.5–4s: substantiate.** Name a relevant authority or show the specific fact, date, or number. Do not stretch mystery with decorative graphics.
- **4–8s: give a first answer.** Explain one concrete fact or relationship. This is a reward, not another unexplained teaser.
- **8–12s: open the next consequential question.** The answer should lead naturally into the mechanism, not an artificial 'wait until the end'.

These are editorial checkpoints, not fixed edit boundaries. Some stories will resolve beats earlier. At seconds 0, 1, 3, 5, 8 and 12, write what a viewer knows and what question remains. Reject a cold open that provides zero educational value by eight seconds.

## Rule 4 — Retention beats: a ladder of rewards

Build the full script as a sequence of *knowledge gains*, not chapters that merely announce themselves:
- Consequence/contradiction → proof → partial explanation → next implication → mechanism → counterpoint/limit → final payoff.
- Every ~5–10 seconds in a typical 60-second video, deliver a concrete fact, verified reveal, or demonstrable causal step. There is **no compulsory cut interval** and no obligation to use suspense at every beat.
- After a question, deliver its answer soon; do not withhold a trivial answer merely for retention.
- Replace generic transitions ('Tapi tunggu dulu', 'Dan bukan cuma itu') with specific bridges ('Masalahnya, dana yang mengikuti indeks harus menyesuaikan portofolio').
- Each beat must justify its time. If it adds neither understanding nor a necessary pause/contrast, cut it.
- Avoid the "second intro" problem: never reintroduce the topic at 8–15 seconds.
- Front-load the strongest *substantiated* idea; place important caveats at the time of the claim, not only at the end.
- Do not promise 'hidden investment winners' when you only have a speculative business-category hypothesis.

### Information density

Use a single dominant idea per composition. Allow one evidence graphic to unfold over several seconds instead of replacing it every 1–2 seconds. Narration and visuals should *co-produce* the idea: the chart moves when the narrator says the changed quantity, not before. Use natural spoken Indonesian; write numbers/dates in voice-friendly form and verify speech pronunciation.

## Rule 5 — Earn visual attention without visual noise

Study the three uploaded 0x100x references as a *discipline*: strong authority/scale openings; incremental counters; visual contradiction; sparse thesis frames; purposeful background flips; mostly quiet frontal cameras; selective historic B-roll and meaningful charts. Do not mimic exact asset designs or pretend mere resemblance causes their performance.

- A strong **thumbnail first frame** with 1 visually dominant subject; minimize initial text (roughly 3–8 memorable words, though clarity controls).
- An animated evidence object in the opening three seconds where the subject warrants it: counter, falling classification marker, signature, document, public figure ID card.
- Alternate **high-density explanation → stark typography/negative space** at true argumentative turns, not on a stopwatch.
- Reserve the hero 3D camera move for the key reveal; early seconds can win with a sharp cut and a legible number.
- Use a **visual callback** later (same number/object returning with changed meaning) when it advances understanding; this can reward attention and create narrative closure.
- Use original, properly licensed real-world imagery when recognition boosts credibility. No generic visual clutter or unlicensed video rips.
- Distinguish the eye-catching main headline from the single-word top microcaption; microcaptions never carry the whole hook.
- Make the final frame answer/echo the first question. Keep CTA and brand ending short; don't end by opening another unfulfilled loop.

## Rule 6 — Retention-first script audit

Before voice generation, complete this checklist with exact quotes and timestamps:
1. **Hook:** Could a stranger immediately say what is at stake?
2. **Evidence:** What proof appears by second 5?
3. **First payoff:** What useful answer do we deliver by second 8–12?
4. **Reason to continue:** Does each answer lead to a genuine next question?
5. **Concrete nouns:** Can a 12-year-old picture the mechanism without financial jargon?
6. **Cut test:** Remove every sentence that repeats the headline, delays explanation, states an empty rhetorical question, or adds irrelevant context.
7. **Promise/payoff alignment:** Does the final reveal actually answer the opening?
8. **Truth test:** Could a viewer reasonably mistake a proposal, prediction, or allegation for completed fact? Correct the first frame and wording.
9. **Silent test:** Mute the first 3 seconds. Is the hook still legible? Mute an explanation beat. Can its diagram still convey the relationship?
10. **Share reason:** Is there a real person for whom this video would be useful, not just sensational?

**Do not animate a script that fails this audit.** Rewrite before moving into expensive motion production.

## Rule 7 — Build Hook A/B variants into the workflow

Prepare **two distinct 0–8-second hook variants** with the same factual body, optimized for different credible framings:
- Variant A: personal/economic consequence.
- Variant B: authority/contradiction/scale.

Export short comparison previews (or contact-sheet/animatic) and review without sound. Use one variant for release. If an actual Instagram A/B or Trial Reels workflow is supported for the account, test in-platform, while recording differing audience distribution/cover/caption conditions. Do not claim a fair randomized experiment unless true. Do not automatically spam the same Reel as multiple public uploads; the user should control release.

## Rule 8 — Treat retention metrics as diagnosis, not decoration

After posting, request/use **actual Instagram Insights** (or connected analytics if authorized), including:
- Video duration, average watch time (seconds), total watch time/plays if exposed.
- Three-second hold / skip / view rate, if available.
- Viewer-retention curve at approximately 1, 3, 5, 8, 15, 30 seconds and at major script reveal points if available.
- Reach, plays/views, follower versus non-follower breakdown, shares/sends, saves, likes and follows, where available.
- Cover/hook variant, topic, posting context and audience segment.

Calculate **diagnostic** watch-time-to-runtime ratio = average watch time / runtime, but do not call it an official Instagram ranking metric. Replay behavior can make ratios exceed 100%. Compare equivalent-length Reels and compare to the channel's own baseline, **not arbitrary universal viral thresholds**. Instagram has publicly identified watch time, likes and sends as important signals; no exact formula or guarantee is known.

**Drop diagnosis:**
- Sharp fall in 0–3s: opening image, consequence, legibility, topical relevance or audience targeting.
- Strong 0–3s but sharp fall at 4–10s: hook overpromised or first payoff delayed.
- Decline in middle: repeated context, jargon, weak causal diagram, pace mismatch.
- Decline before end: conclusion already delivered, irrelevant historical detour, too much CTA.
- Strong watch time but weak sends: useful but not distinctive/share-worthy, or wrong recipient framing.

Treat these as hypotheses to test. Without the actual retention graph, do not diagnose the hook as proven cause. Produce a postmortem proposing **one variable to change next episode** so iterations are interpretable.

## Rule 9 — Delivery and QC now require retention evidence

For every episode save:
- `retention-brief.md` — target viewer, stakes, specific payoff, share reason.
- `hooks.md` — five candidate cold opens, rubric, selected hook and one alternative.
- `cold-open-storyboard.md` — second-by-second visual/audio/evidence plan for 0–12s.
- `script.md`, `storyboard.md`, `transition-map.md`, `sources.md`.
- `qa-report.md` — factual and technical QC plus first-12s editorial review.
- `performance-review.md` — after *real metrics* exist; mark `NOT YET MEASURED` before posting, never invent analytics.

**Critical rejection gates:**
- Hook's promised consequence cannot be supported.
- First frame lacks an understandable subject/stake.
- Opening is only an animated logo or dramatic but meaningless 3D animation.
- No tangible proof or educational return in the first ~8–12 seconds.
- The script repeats context or asks generic questions to pad runtime.
- The animation is a deck of disconnected diagrams rather than a causally explained story.
- The final answer does not fulfill the first question.

**The ultimate standard:** Make the first frame irresistible *because it matters*, the first 10 seconds rewarding *because something is learned*, and the next 50 seconds worth watching *because each answer leads to a deeper real consequence*. No fabricated mystery. No promise of virality.

## Appendix — Cryptonite hook examples (illustrative, verify fresh facts before use)

**MSCI proposal:**
- Weak: "Indonesia terancam dikeluarkan dari indeks saham global MSCI."
- Consequence: "Kalau MSCI mencoret saham Indonesia dari indeks utamanya, sebagian dana indeks global harus menyesuaikan portofolionya."
- Contradiction: "MSCI menilai turun ke Frontier saja belum cukup untuk Indonesia." (Explain clearly: Standalone is outside Emerging and Frontier index families, **not literally a formal third rung**.)
- On-screen first frame: `FRONTIER SAJA TAK CUKUP?` + small `USULAN MSCI · BELUM FINAL`.
- First payoff (within 8 seconds): "MSCI justru mengusulkan Standalone—di luar dua kelompok indeks tersebut." Explain why that affects some index trackers, and that outcome awaits a final decision.

**AI/crypto warning:**
- Weak: "Bisakah AI menghancurkan cryptocurrency?"
- Consequence: "Peneliti Ethereum khawatir AI bisa menemukan cara membongkar kunci dompet kripto. Tapi belum ada bukti serangan itu berhasil."
- Contradiction: "Ancaman yang dikhawatirkan bukan menunggu komputer kuantum—melainkan temuan matematika oleh AI."
- First payoff: public/private key relationship and the exact uncertainty, *not* a fake cryptographic breach.
