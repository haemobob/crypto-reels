November 19, 2026 11:30 Shenzhen At the end of the meeting, the Russia-China-US Heads of State Declaration toward Peace was adopted. * * * Taking part in the trilateral meeting on the Russian side were Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov Lavrov Sergei Minister of Foreign Affairs of the Russian Federation , Deputy Prime Minister – Finance Minister Anton Siluanov Siluanov Anton Deputy Prime Minister of the Government of the Russian Federation – Minister of Finance of the Russian Federation , Deputy Chief of Staff of the Presidential Executive Office Maxim Oreshkin Oreshkin Maxim Deputy Chief of Staff of the Presidential Executive Office , Presidential Aide Yury Ushakov Ushakov Yury Aide to the President , and Defence Minister Andrei Belousov Belousov Andrei Defence Minister of the Russian Federation . The Chinese side was represented by Member of the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee, Head of the General Office of the CPC Central Committee Cai Qi, Member of the Political Bureau, Director of the Office of the CPC Central Commission for Foreign Affairs, Foreign Minister Wang Yi, Executive Vice Minister of Foreign Affairs Ma Zhaoxu, Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the People’s Republic of China to the United States of America Xie Feng, and Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the People’s Republic of China to the Russian Federation Zhang Hanhui. Taking part in the meeting on the American side were Secretary of State, National Security Adviser to the US President Marco Rubio, Secretary of the Treasury Scott Bessent, Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick, White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, and US Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary to the People's Republic of China David Perdue. * * * President of the People’s Republic of China Xi Jinping Xi Jinping President of People's Republic of China (retranslated) : I very much welcome a trilateral meeting between the leaders of China, Russia and the United States. The first trilateral summit in this format is truly a landmark event. The main topic of our discussion today is maintaining world peace and promoting strategic stability in relations between major powers. Peace is an eternal aspiration of humanity. Our planet has seen over 10,000 wars, and the two largest wars brought unprecedented suffering to humanity. There are more than 60 smouldering local conflicts throughout the world. History teaches us that good relations between major powers make the world a safer place, while confrontation between them plunges it into chaos. More than 80 years ago, our three countries, as the mainstay of the global anti-fascist front, fought side by side and achieved the Great Victory, thereby protecting peaceful skies overhead. History offers us the most valuable lessons. We must preserve historical memory and build the future on its foundations. The world today no longer consists of divided islands. I frequently say that humanity is a global village. We prosper and suffer together as a single whole, sharing the same fate. In this global village, major powers bear a special responsibility. Good-neighbourly relations between global powers are not merely one option among many; they are the only viable choice. In recent years, we have been actively working to strengthen Chinese-Russian relations of comprehensive partnership and strategic cooperation in the new era, while also building constructive, strategic and stable Chinese-American relations. We are promoting an architecture of relations among major powers in the spirit of peaceful coexistence, comprehensive stability and balanced development. This benefits not only our three countries but the entire world. The world cannot be described as tranquil amid the tectonic global shifts unseen in centuries. It is in times of adversity that a sense of duty is put to the test. As leading world powers and permanent members of the UN Security Council, our three countries must set a worthy example, working jointly to respond to global challenges, upholding peace and stability throughout the planet, and increasing our contribution to the cause of human progress. Colleagues, I would now like to hear your views. President Putin, you have the floor. Please. President of Russia Vladimir Putin: President Xi Jinping, President of the United States Donald Trump, First of all, I would like to express my gratitude to the Chinese side and to the President of the People’s Republic of China for organising today’s meeting. This is the first time such a meeting has been held, bringing together the leaders of three countries that are all permanent members of the UN Security Council: Russia, China and the United States. Our three countries are united not only by our status as victors in World War II but, more importantly, by our shared responsibility for maintaining international peace and security. We were among the founding members of the United Nations and therefore bear a special responsibility for preserving and strengthening the system of international law that took shape with the establishment of this universal organisation, a system based on the UN Charter and the principles enshrined in it. Of course, we should also discuss global and regional economic ties, as well as food and energy security. Our countries have many common interests when it comes to resolving regional conflicts, including, of course, the conflict in Ukraine. I discussed this issue yesterday with the President of China, and today, at the initiative of the President of the United States, we devoted considerable attention to it during our bilateral meeting. We also share common interests in countering contemporary threats and challenges, including international terrorism, transnational organised crime and other such threats. For my part, I am convinced that Russia, China, and the United States, and our peoples, can make a significant contribution to strengthening security and stability around the world if we work together constructively, in a spirit of partnership, on the basis of mutual respect, equality and a balance of interests. Thank you. Xi Jinping: Thank you. The floor goes to President Trump. President of the United States Donald Trump Trump Donald President of the United States of America : Well, thank you very much, President Xi, and for so warmly welcoming me back to China. I’ve been here a number of times over the years. It’s incredible what’s taken place. But this is the second time this year, following our beautiful state visit to Beijing in May. That was quite something. A lot of things were accomplished, actually. It’s an honour to be here in Shenzhen, and I want to congratulate President Xi on hosting a very successful APEC summit. It was very successful. Tremendous thought given in that room. Very, very good. I’m delighted that this trip has also given us the opportunity for this first-ever trilateral meeting between the leaders of the United States, Russia and China. And today so in that sense, and I think in lots of other ways, we are really making history. The three of us have a great deal to discuss, and we’ve already discussed a lot. As presidents of three of the world’s largest countries, we have a special responsibility to maintain open, respectful, and productive dialogue on issues impacting not just our people, but the entire planet. Too often, past leaders avoided talking to nations with whom they had disagreements. And we feel the opposite, and we think that we are sure about that. No problems were ever solved under the old school refusing to talk about them. So, no peace was ever forged by declining opportunities for engagement and speech. Together, we are choosing a different course. A historic summit offers us the chance to seek out new opportunities and approaches on a range of very important issues, most of them everybody in this room knows, and hopefully to create a better future for all of us, a very, very much better future and safer future. As the world’s three largest nuclear powers, by far, the United States, Russia, and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth, and also to lessen the risk that those weapons will ever be used. The power is unbelievable, unthinkable. In addition to exploring the potential for arms reduction in our own countries, and I must say, it’s, quite frankly, a very hard thing to do because once you have the nuclear weapons, it’s very hard to not have them. It’s very hard to give them up as the leader of a country. Something that’s very difficult to say because it’s the way life works. You know, you have thousands of weapons, and you’re supposed to give them up, and yet you have countries, in addition to us, in much smaller numbers, but they have nuclear weapons also. So, it’s not an easy thing to do, give them up. But it’s also vital that we cooperate to combat the threat of nuclear proliferation in other countries around the world. That’s what we’re doing in Iran. We’re not going to let Iran have a nuclear weapon. And to that end, today we’ll be talking about all of the problems that are really being discussed in the world. We are going to be discussing the war between Russia and Ukraine, and we already have. President Putin and I have had a long discussion today for quite a while. That’s what, pretty much what the discussion was about. And I think things are happening. I think that he, and I hope the other side, would like to see an end to that war. In another area of potential cooperation between us, I’ll be suggesting ways to protect our nations’ sovereignty from rogue globalist organisations such as the International Criminal Court, which has gotten out of control, which I believe we all agree has really strayed far beyond its jurisdiction. So today marks the first-ever trilateral meeting between our countries, and it’s an honour to be with Vladimir. It’s an honour to be with you, Mr President. What a job you’ve done. Think of it. This is 40 years, and I think most of it took place in the last 20 or 25 years, and they have a major city, one of the biggest cities anywhere in the world, and it’s flawless. And what an achievement that is. So, it’s an honour for me to be with you. It’s an honour for me to be with you. And let’s see if we can get something done. Thank you. Thank you very much.
