# Research — trader-1-persen (checked 11 Nov 2026)

## 5W1H
- **What:** a viral clip (GoddyFX on X, 11 Nov 2026) shows three stick-figure "traders" shooting hoops, labelled 93%, 6% and 1% of traders; only the 1% keeps shooting until it scores: "Be among the 1% who refuse to give up." The percentages are not sourced.
- **The evidence we use instead:** Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (FEA-USP working paper 2019-47, 11 Dec 2019; updated June 2020). Data from Brazil's securities regulator (CVM) on **every** individual who began day trading mini-Ibovespa index futures in 2013–2015: **19,646 people**.
- **Why it matters:** "don't give up" is the standard pitch of trading courses and signal groups; the one large dataset that follows every beginner says persistence did not make day traders profitable.
- **How:** show the share in net profit by how long people kept going.

## The numbers (S1 abstract, S2 paper text quoted in a summary, S3 summary of the June 2020 update)
| Days day-traded | People | With positive net profit | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 day | 1,111 | 29.8% | Fact (S2) |
| 2–50 days | 9,978 | 15.5% | Fact (S2) |
| 51–100 days | 3,100 | 8.9% | Fact (S2) |
| 101–200 days | 2,738 | 6.8% | Fact (S2) |
| 201–300 days | 1,168 | 5.4% | Fact (S2) |
| > 300 days | 1,551 | 3.0% (so 97% lost money) | Fact (S1, S2, S3) |

- Of the 1,551 who persisted > 300 days: 17 (1.1%) earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage (US$16/day); 8 (0.5%) more than a bank teller's starting salary (US$54/day); the best earned US$310/day on average, with a standard deviation of US$2,560 (S2, S3). The 2019 abstract says 0.4% for the bank-teller figure (S1); later versions 0.5%. Not used on screen.
- "No evidence of learning by day trading" (S1, S3).
- Net = after exchange and brokerage fees; income taxes and costs such as courses and platforms are not deducted (S3).

## Limits (stated on screen)
- Brazil, equity index futures, 2013–2015: **not crypto**. The sample may not represent other countries or assets (S3's caveat).
- It describes outcomes, not causes: it doesn't say quitting makes anyone profitable.
