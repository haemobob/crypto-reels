Photo: Spicypepper999 / Wikimedia Commons (CC0) Key Facts Silver closed at its lowest in at least six months, with the silver-tracking price at US$51.94 an ounce, down 3.66% in the Thursday session. Gold fell 1.14% to US$4,008 an ounce, its second straight losing session, as markets priced an 83% chance of a Federal Reserve hike in December. The Federal Reserve held its policy rate at 3.75% to 4.00%, but two policymakers voted for an immediate 25-basis-point increase, reinforcing higher-for-longer rate expectations. Peruvian miner Buenaventura fell 3.57% in New York and Mexico City-listed Grupo México slipped roughly 0.7% as the precious metals pullback hit Latin American producers. Silver is down 54.7% from its January 28 high of US$114.59 an ounce, a brutal bear market that has erased more than half the metal’s value in nine months. The 10-year US Treasury yield eased to 4.94% from 5.02%, and the dollar index fell 0.33%, moves that would normally support bullion but were outweighed by rate-hike fears. Today’s Focus Silver has collapsed to its lowest close in at least six months, ending Thursday at US$51.94 an ounce, down 3.66% on the session. Gold fell 1.14% to US$4,008 an ounce, its second consecutive daily loss. The trigger was the Federal Reserve. Policymakers left rates at 3.75% to 4.00%, but two officials pushed for an immediate quarter-point hike, and futures markets now put roughly an 83% probability on a December move. Higher expected borrowing costs lift the inflation-adjusted return on interest-bearing assets, draining the attraction of metals that pay no yield.
